## Quick answer

Realty Income (O) put up a **better-than-expected Q3 2026**. The stock trades around **$58.82** with a **5.52% dividend yield**, its cash flow (AFFO) improved both quarter over quarter and year over year, and it kept its streak of raising the dividend **every quarter for 29 years**. For income investors, it still does exactly what it promises.

*All figures as of November 15, 2026.*

## Realty Income stock price and dividend yield

As I write this, Realty Income trades at **$58.82 per share**.

The slide that started in September has calmed down, and the stock is edging back up. Sharp rate hikes in the US had weighed on the price, but a solid Q3 report has put it back on a recovery path.

The current dividend yield is **5.52%** (before taxes).

My takeaway from this quarter: Realty Income hasn't lost its focus. It's still quietly running the same business it always has — and I mean that as a compliment.

## The company that only cares about dividends

I say this with love: Realty Income is a company that only knows dividends.

Management is obsessed with two things — real estate and paying shareholders. They don't seem very interested in pushing the stock price up.

Most CEOs come on stage and tell you how amazing their company is and how the stock is going to soar. Realty Income doesn't do that kind of short-term hype. As a shareholder, that can feel a little underwhelming sometimes. But instead of marketing, it proves itself the slow way: **by growing the dividend, year after year.**

## 116 consecutive quarterly dividend increases

Realty Income has raised its dividend for **116 quarters in a row**. That's an increase *every single quarter for 29 years*.

Plenty of S&P 500 companies pay good dividends, but a record like this puts it in a special class. Realty Income is a member of the **S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats** — the index of S&P 500 companies that have grown their dividends for 25+ consecutive years.

It has also declared **676 consecutive monthly dividends**. Through more than 50 years and countless economic crises, it has kept its promise to pay shareholders every month.

## Q3 2026 earnings: strong cash generation (AFFO)

When you look at a REIT like Realty Income, the single most important thing is **how much cash it generates**. Most shareholders own it for a steady, rising dividend. The moment the market suspects the dividend won't keep growing — or might be cut — the stock falls hard.

The key metric for this is **AFFO (Adjusted Funds From Operations)**. You don't need to memorize the term; just think of it as "the cash available to pay dividends."

In Q3, Realty Income's AFFO **rose meaningfully from the previous quarter** and was **solidly ahead of the same quarter last year**. That eased worries that high interest rates would squeeze the business, and the stock has settled back into the $58–59 range.

## Diversification: now including data centers

There's another reason I like Realty Income. It's no longer just a landlord for convenience stores and supermarkets. It has been diversifying into **data centers** and **gaming** (casinos and resorts).

In Q3, it also launched a data center venture with **Cloud Capital** — its second official data center deal, after the 2024 project with **Digital Realty**. It's already meaningful enough to show up in the numbers: about **1% of total revenue** now comes from data centers.

On top of that, its **joint venture with KKR** is operating in Europe, in Italy and Spain — two of Realty Income's main markets there.

## Key takeaways

- **Price & yield:** ~$58.82 per share, 5.52% dividend yield.
- **Dividend streak:** 116 consecutive quarterly raises (29 years), 676 consecutive monthly dividends, Dividend Aristocrat.
- **Cash flow:** AFFO up versus last quarter and last year — the dividend looks well supported.
- **Growth angles:** data centers (~1% of revenue), gaming properties, and a KKR joint venture in Europe.
- **My view:** not a stock for fast gains, but one of the most reliable monthly income payers you can own.
